    1018 cold-drawn vs hot-rolled (and A36): tolerances, residual stress and machining

    1018 cold-drawn (CRS) or hot-rolled (HRS) bar, or A36: finish, tolerances, strength, residual stresses that bow parts machined on one side, case hardening and welding.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    1018 (SAE/AISI, about 0.18% carbon) is the most common bar steel in machining. It’s sold mainly cold drawn / cold finished (“CRS”) and sometimes hot rolled (“HRS”). A36 is a structural standard: you find it in hot-rolled flat bar and shapes. Chemically the three are close, but not when machining.

    Comparison

    Criterion1018 cold drawn1018 hot rolledA36 hot rolled
    SurfaceSmooth, cleanMill scaleMill scale
    Dimensional tolerancesTight (ready-to-use bar)WideWide
    StrengthHigher (cold work from drawing)LowerSpecified by A36
    Residual stressHighLowLow
    CompositionControlled by SAE 1018SameA36 mainly controls mechanical properties
    UsesShafts, pins, precise machined partsHeavily machined parts, welded partsStructure, welded fabrication

    The trap: cold-drawing stresses

    Drawing leaves internal stresses. When you remove material from one side only (keyway, pocket, asymmetric facing), the bar bows. Common fixes:

    1. Rough both sides, let it rest (or stress relieve thermally), then finish.
    2. Machine symmetrically where possible.
    3. Choose hot-rolled or A36 for heavily pocketed parts, or stress-relieved / ground bar.

    Machining

    • Cold-drawn 1018 machines decently but can be gummy (long chips); production screw machining prefers resulfurized steels (see 12L14 and 1215 (free-machining)).
    • Published machinability ratings (B1112 = 100 reference): about 70–78% for 1018, per distributor tables.
    • Chip breakers, adequate feed and coolant improve chip control.

    Case hardening and welding

    • 1018 is a classic carburizing steel: tough core, hard surface.
    • It welds well; leaded or resulfurized steels weld poorly.

    Ordering checklist

    • Grade and condition (1018 CD, 1018 HR, A36)
    • Shape and size (round, square, flat, hex)
    • Required tolerance (drawn vs ground/polished)
    • Length and cut-to-length by the supplier
    • Compare published prices in the price comparison tool

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Deep keyway in cold-drawn 1018 without stress reliefBowed shaft
    Using A36 for a precision turned partExtra stock to remove, abrasive scale
    Assuming A36 = 1018Composition and properties not equivalent
    Welding 12L14 instead of 1018Porosity, cracks

    FAQ

    Why is cold-drawn 1018 stronger than hot-rolled?

    Cold drawing work-hardens the bar; strength rises, ductility drops a little, and internal stresses appear.

    Can 1018 be through-hardened?

    Not effectively: too little carbon. It’s carburized to harden the surface, or you choose an alloy steel (see 4140 vs 4340).
