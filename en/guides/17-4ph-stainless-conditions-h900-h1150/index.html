    17-4PH stainless: conditions A, H900, H1025 and H1150, machining and aging

    What 17-4PH (UNS S17400) conditions mean, when to machine (in condition A, before aging), dimensional contraction on aging, and which condition to choose between hardness and toughness.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page

    17-4PH (UNS S17400, “type 630”) is a precipitation-hardening martensitic stainless: it ships solution-treated (condition A), is machined in that state, then aged at low temperature to reach the required hardness. The condition name gives the aging temperature in °F.

    The conditions

    ConditionAging (per producer data sheets)Character
    ASolution-treated, not agedAs-delivered; machines like a 302/304 austenitic stainless per AK Steel
    H900900 °F (482 °C), 1 h, air coolMaximum hardness and strength, lowest toughness, hardest to machine
    H10251025 °F (552 °C), 4 hCompromise
    H10751075 °F (580 °C), 4 hTougher
    H11501150 °F (621 °C), 4 hToughest and easiest to machine of the aged conditions
    H1150M, H1150DDouble treatmentsMaximum toughness, special uses

    Exact properties per condition (yield, hardness, impact) are in Cleveland-Cliffs (formerly AK Steel) and Carpenter (Custom 630) data sheets.

    When to machine

    1. Rough and finish in condition A if the tolerance allows for contraction.
    2. Age to the required condition.
    3. Grind or lightly finish tight dimensions after aging.

    Producers state a contraction on aging from condition A: about 0.00045 mm/mm for H900, 0.00053 mm/mm for H1025 and 0.0022 mm/mm for H1150 (Armco 17-4 PH data sheet). On a 100 mm part, that’s roughly 0.05 mm to 0.22 mm depending on condition — plan for it in stock allowances.

    Machining in practice

    • Condition A: tools and conditions of a 304 stainless (see Stainless: 303, 304, 316) — firm feed, no rubbing.
    • H900: high hardness, fast tool wear; carbide, reduced speeds, rigid setup.
    • H1150: easier than H900; a common reason to choose H1150 when strength is sufficient.

    Choosing the condition

    NeedCondition often chosen
    Maximum hardness and strength, little impactH900
    Strength / toughness compromiseH1025
    Toughness, stress-corrosion cracking resistance, easier machiningH1150

    The designer owns the choice: the condition must be on the drawing (e.g. “17-4PH, AMS 5643, H1025”).

    Checklist

    • Delivery condition (A) confirmed by the certificate
    • Final condition on the drawing
    • Allowance for contraction and finishing
    • Heat treatment by a qualified subcontractor, with report
    • Hardness checked after aging if required

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Finishing to size in condition A without allowing for contractionTight dimensions out of tolerance after aging
    Machining in H900 “because it’s simpler”Tools consumed, long cycle times
    Mixing up 17-4PH and 304 in stockPart can’t harden or wrong corrosion behaviour
    Not stating the condition on the POMaterial arrives in an unexpected state

    FAQ

    Is 17-4PH magnetic?

    Yes: it is a martensitic stainless, unlike annealed 304/316.

    Can it be welded?

    Yes with a suitable procedure, often followed by heat treatment; see the producer’s data sheet.
