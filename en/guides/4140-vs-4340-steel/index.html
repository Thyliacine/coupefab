    4140 vs 4340 steel: differences, conditions (annealed, prehardened) and machining

    4140 (chromium-molybdenum) and 4340 (with nickel): hardenability, toughness, section sizes, delivery conditions (annealed, prehardened 28–32 HRC), machining, welding with preheat, and how to choose.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    4140 and 4340 are the two most common alloy steels in machine shops for shafts, pins, tooling and stressed parts. Both contain chromium and molybdenum; 4340 adds nickel (about 1.65 to 2.0% per the AISI/SAE composition), which raises hardenability (through-hardness in heavy sections) and toughness.

    Comparison

    Criterion41404340
    Alloying elementsCr, MoNi, Cr, Mo
    HardenabilityGoodHigher: better through-hardness in large sections
    Toughness / fatigueGoodHigher at equal strength
    CostLowerHigher
    MachinabilityGood when annealedA bit harder, more tool wear
    Typical usesShafts, pins, tool holders, fixtures, medium partsFatigue/impact-critical parts, heavy sections, aerospace, heavy tooling

    Delivery conditions

    ConditionDescriptionFor whom
    AnnealedSoftest, best machiningParts to be quenched and tempered after machining
    Pre-hardened (“4140 HT”)Quenched and tempered at the mill, often around 28–32 HRCFinished parts with no later heat treatment
    NormalizedUniform structurePer specification

    Machining approach

    1. Pre-hardened: machine straight to size; carbide tools, more conservative conditions than annealed (see Feeds and speeds).
    2. Annealed + heat treat after: rough, leave stock, quench and temper, then grind or finish tight dimensions (possible distortion in hardening).
    3. Stress relieve between roughing and finishing if the part is long or asymmetric.

    Welding

    Both steels are hardenable: without precautions the heat-affected zone can harden and crack. Procedures usually call for preheat, interpass temperature control, matching filler metal and sometimes post-weld heat treatment. Follow a qualified procedure; see Weld preparation.

    Choosing

    • Medium part, normal loading, tight budget → 4140 (often pre-hardened).
    • Large section that must through-harden, critical impact or fatigue → 4340.
    • When in doubt: the designer’s specification (and the cited standard) wins.

    Ordering checklist

    • Grade and condition (4140 annealed, 4140 HT, 4340 annealed…)
    • Required hardness and how it’s verified
    • Mill certificate
    • Allowance if heat treatment follows
    • Compare published prices in the price comparison tool

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Welding 4140 without preheatCold cracking
    Finishing to size before hardeningDistortion out of tolerance
    Ordering “4140” without a conditionAnnealed stock delivered for a part that needed to be hard
    Using 1018 speedsRapid tool wear

    FAQ

    Can pre-hardened 4140 be hardened further?

    Yes with a new full heat treatment, but you buy pre-hardened precisely to avoid heat treating after machining.

    4140 or 4130?

    4130 has less carbon: better weldability (chassis tubing), lower achievable hardness.
