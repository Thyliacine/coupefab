    Materials · aluminum

    Aluminum in the shop: 5052, 6061 and 6063, tempers (T6, H32) and how to cut, bend, weld and machine them

    Which grade for which part: 5052-H32 for bent sheet, 6061-T6 for machining and structure, 6063 for extrusions. Temper designations, bending, welding, plasma/laser cutting and machining, linked to published prices.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    Shop aluminum often comes down to three grades: 5052 (sheet to bend), 6061 (plate, bar, machining, structure) and 6063 (extrusions, architectural). Choosing correctly avoids cracked bends, parts that are too soft and extra cost.

    Reading a designation: alloy + temper

    PartExampleMeaning
    Series5xxxMagnesium alloy, not heat-treatable (strain-hardened)
    Series6xxxMagnesium-silicon alloy, heat-treatable
    H temperH32Strain-hardened then stabilized (5052-H32 is the most common sheet temper)
    T temperT6Solution heat-treated and artificially aged (6061’s strongest temper)
    T temperT5, T52Cooled from the press then aged (common for extruded 6063)
    O temperOAnnealed, softest and most formable

    The three grades compared

    Criterion5052-H326061-T66063-T5/T6
    Typical useBent sheet, enclosures, tanks, marineMachined plate and bar, structural partsExtrusions, frames, architecture, decorative anodized parts
    BendingExcellent, tight radii possiblePoor in T6: risk of cracking, generous radii or O/T4 temperGood for extrusions depending on temper
    WeldingVery goodGood, but strength loss in the weld zoneGood, same remark
    MachiningGummierVery good (clean chips)Fair
    AnodizingGoodGoodExcellent (decorative finish)
    StrengthMediumHighest of the threeMedium

    For exact properties (yield, elongation), use producer data sheets (Hydro, etc.) and mill certificates.

    Cutting aluminum

    • Plasma: works, with aluminum charts (air or specific gases depending on the source). Edge less clean than on steel; see Reading cut charts.
    • Fiber laser: clean cut with nitrogen; reflective material, follow the manufacturer’s recipes. See Laser: dross and burning.
    • Waterjet: no heat-affected zone; ideal for thick plate or sensitive parts. See Waterjet.
    • Router: very effective on sheet and plate with single- or two-flute aluminum cutters; see CNC router.

    Bending aluminum

    • Grain direction matters: bend across the rolling direction when possible.
    • 6061-T6 bends poorly at tight radii; ask for 5052-H32 for bent sheet, or 6061 in a softer temper.
    • K-factor and springback differ from steel; see Bending: K-factor and deduction and Press brake.

    Machining aluminum

    • 2- or 3-flute end mills, polished flutes, high speeds: follow tool makers’ data (see Feeds and speeds).
    • The enemy is built-up edge (welded chip): lubrication or air blast, chip evacuation.
    • 6061-T6 and 7075 machine better than 5052 or soft tempers.

    Ordering

    • Grade and temper (e.g. 5052-H32, 6061-T6511 for stress-relieved stretched bar)
    • Thickness in decimals (aluminum gauges follow Brown & Sharpe, see Sheet metal gauges)
    • Protective film if the face is visible
    • Mill certificate if the part is structural
    • Compare published prices in the price comparison tool

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Bending 6061-T6 at a tight radiusCracks on the outside of the bend
    Using the steel gauge tableWrong thickness
    Welding a “structural” 6061-T6 part without accounting for it in designWeaker weld zone
    Same brushes as for steelContamination, weld porosity

    FAQ

    6061 or 6063 for a frame?

    6063 for appearance extrusions (anodizing, complex shapes); 6061 if strength comes first. Both come as tube and profiles.

    Can 5052 be heat-treated?

    No: it doesn’t harden by heat treatment, only by strain hardening (H tempers).
