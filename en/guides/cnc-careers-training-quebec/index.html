    CNC careers and training in Quebec: DEP machining, ASP CNC, DEC and health and safety

    Paths to become a machinist, CNC operator or programmer in Quebec: DEP 5223 (1,800 h), ASP 5224 (885 h), mechanical engineering technology DEC, on-the-job training, health and safety training obligations (CNESST) and published wages.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    Quebec trains its machinists mainly through vocational training (school service centres) and its technicians through CEGEPs. Here are the paths, published durations and what the employer must provide for health and safety.

    Training paths

    ProgramLevelDurationFor whom
    DEP 5223 – Techniques d’usinage (machining techniques)Vocational diploma1,800 hoursBecoming a machinist: blueprint reading, metrology, manual lathe and mill, intro to CNC
    ASP 5224 – Usinage sur machines-outils à commande numérique (CNC machining)Vocational specialization885 hoursAfter the DEP (or related experience): programming, setup and operation of CNC lathes and machining centres
    DEC – Mechanical engineering technologyCEGEP diploma3 yearsTechnician: design, drafting, CAM, methods, quality control
    On-the-job training—VariesOperators trained in-house; manufacturers (Haas, Mastercam, etc.) also run courses

    Durations from published program sheets (AdmissionFP, training centres). Admissions go through your school service centre’s regional platform.

    The jobs

    JobWhat they doTypical training
    CNC operatorLoads parts, runs programs, checks dimensionsDEP or on-the-job
    Machinist / setterSets tools and fixtures, sets offsets, adapts programsDEP 5223 + ASP 5224
    CNC / CAM programmerPrograms in CAM (Mastercam, Fusion, SolidCAM…), optimizesASP 5224 or DEC + experience
    Laser / plasma operatorNesting, recipe tuning, cut qualityOn-the-job, manufacturer courses
    Inspector / metrologistDimensional inspection, CMM, reportsDEP/DEC + metrology training

    The machinist occupation is NOC 72100; wages published by Job Bank are summarized in Shop hourly rates (Quebec).

    Health and safety: what the employer must do

    • The Act respecting occupational health and safety requires the employer to inform, train and supervise workers, and to provide protective equipment.
    • The Regulation respecting occupational health and safety (RSST) covers machine guarding (guards, safety devices) and lockout, among other things.
    • CNESST publishes prevention guides (machine safety, welding fumes, noise). See Safety and CNESST.

    Checklist for a new CNC shop employee

    • Hazard training for each machine (guards, emergency stop)
    • Lockout procedure explained and practised
    • PPE provided: glasses, hearing protection, shoes, gloves as the task requires (never gloves near a rotating spindle)
    • Blueprint reading and measuring instruments checked
    • Supervision until documented autonomy

    FAQ

    Do you need a DEP to work on a CNC?

    Not legally, but most employers ask for it for machinist roles; operator roles are often open with on-the-job training.

    Is the ASP useful if I already program in CAM?

    It structures machine setup, offsets and manual programming (G-code), useful even with CAM: see G-code & M-code and Work offsets (G54).
