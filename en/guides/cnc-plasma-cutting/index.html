    CNC plasma cutting: how it works and how to start right

    How the plasma arc works, air versus high-definition plasma, torch height control (THC), water or downdraft table, software chain and settings: the overview to choose well and nail your first cuts.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page
    1. The full chain
    2. Air or high definition?
    3. The settings that matter

    5. Water table or downdraft table

    Plasma cutting melts metal with an electric arc constricted in an ionized gas, then blows the molten metal out with the jet. It cuts every conductive metal (steel, stainless, aluminum) quickly, for a machine cost far below a laser. Its weak points: a wider kerf and a slight bevel.

    Air or high definition?

    Air plasma (Powermax type)High definition / precision (XPR type)
    GasCompressed airOxygen, nitrogen, mixes by material
    QualityGood on thin and medium plateReduced bevel, better holes
    CostAffordable machine and consumablesIndustrial investment
    Typical shopSmall shops, general fabricationProduction, precision parts

    The settings that matter

    • Current (amperage) and a matching consumable set: picked from the chart by thickness.
    • Speed: too slow = bottom dross and wide kerf; too fast = trailing arc, hard dross, bevel.
    • Cut height, held by THC (torch height control by arc voltage).
    • Pierce height and delay, and IHS (initial height sensing).
    • Clean, dry air at the manual’s pressure.

    Everything starts from the maker’s cut chart: see Kerf and cut quality and Lead-ins and pierce.

    THC in two sentences

    Arc voltage is proportional to arc length: when the torch gets closer to the plate, voltage drops. THC raises or lowers Z to hold the target voltage (Langmuir describes this principle precisely for its LS-THC). It must be locked in corners and small holes, where speed drops.

    Water table or downdraft table

    Water tableDowndraft table
    FumesPartly trapped by waterCaptured by a dust collector
    DistortionCools the plateNo cooling
    UpkeepDraining, sludge, freezing in an unheated buildingFilters, combustible dust risk (aluminum)

    Caution

    Cutting aluminum over a water table can produce hydrogen that collects under the plate: power source and table makers publish warnings (water agitation, venting). Read your equipment’s.

    First cuts: the method

    1. Pick a common thickness (for example 3–6 mm mild steel).
    2. Use the exact chart row: current, consumables, speed, heights, delay.
    3. Cut a measuring coupon (square + hole) — method in the Kerf guide.
    4. Set kerf in CAM, recut.
    5. Tune speed in small steps by reading the dross — see Plasma troubleshooting.
    6. Save the settings that work in the CAM tool library.

    FAQ

    How thick can it cut?

    Makers distinguish pierce capacity, production cut capacity and severance capacity (slow, poor-quality cut). For a CNC table, look at pierce and production capacity, not severance.

    Does plasma replace a laser?

    For many structural and general fabrication parts, yes. For small precise holes, fine detail and bevel-free edges, fiber laser does better. See Fiber laser.
