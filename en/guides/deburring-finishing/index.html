    Deburring and finishing cut parts: dross, burrs, edges and oxide

    What to remove (dross, burr, sharp edge, cutting oxide), in what order, with which method (hammer, belt sander, brushes, tumbling, wide-belt machine), and how to specify it on a drawing.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page

    2. What to remove and how

    Cutting nearly always leaves something to clean up: dross (resolidified metal under the part), a burr (small lip), a sharp edge that cuts hands and holds paint poorly, and sometimes cutting oxide on the edge. Deburring takes time — it must be priced in the quote (see Quoting cut parts) and reduced at the source with good cut settings.

    First: reduce it at the source

    • Plasma: speed, height and consumables per the chart; see Plasma troubleshooting.
    • Laser: nozzle, centering, focus, gas; see Laser: dross and burning.
    • A good cut needs only a light pass; a bad cut can cost more to deburr than to cut.

    What to remove and how

    DefectCommon methodNotes
    Heavy dross (plasma, oxyfuel)Chipping hammer, chisel, impact deslagging stationFinishing equipment makers recommend removing slag before grinding so abrasives don’t wear out
    Fine burr (laser, punching)Belt sander, wide-belt machine, rotary brushesOne or two passes depending on the machine
    Sharp edge → broken or rounded edgeBrushes, vibratory finishing, file, edge deburring toolSpecify the radius or chamfer wanted
    Oxide on the edge (O₂ cut)Abrasive brushing, grindingImportant before powder coating or welding per the procedure
    Heat tint on stainlessPickling/passivation per good practice, dedicated brushingStainless-only tools

    Equipment by volume

    VolumeTypical equipment
    A few partsFile, grinder with flap disc, handheld belt sander
    Small batchesStationary belt sander, edge deburring tool, vibratory finishing
    ProductionWide-belt machine with brushes (deburring and edge rounding, one or both sides depending on model)

    Specify it on the drawing

    • “Break edges” with no value invites interpretation; prefer a maximum chamfer or radius (e.g. “edges broken 0.2–0.5 mm” if that is your requirement).
    • Call out faces where no burr is acceptable (bearing faces, visible faces).
    • State whether cutting oxide must be removed (paint, welding).

    Pre-shipping checklist

    • No dross that comes off by hand
    • Edges per the drawing (radius/chamfer)
    • Visible faces free of deburring scratches if required
    • Oxide removed if the part goes to paint
    • Stainless parts handled with dedicated tools

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Sanding heavy drossAbrasives worn in minutes, parts heated
    Same brushes for steel and stainlessContamination, rust on stainless
    Not pricing deburringMargin lost on the job
    Over-rounding edges on precision partsFunctional dimensions out of tolerance

    FAQ

    Is vibratory finishing suitable for large parts?

    It suits small parts in bulk; large sheets go better through a wide-belt machine or by hand.

    Should parts going to powder coat be deburred?

    Generally yes: powder pulls back from sharp edges (edge effect) and oxide can hurt adhesion. Check with your coater.
