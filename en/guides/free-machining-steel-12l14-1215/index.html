    Free-machining steels 12L14 and 1215: machinability, limits and when to avoid them

    Why 12L14 (leaded) and 1215 (resulfurized) machine much faster than 1018, and why you shouldn’t weld them or use them everywhere. Comparison and selection.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page

    Free-machining steels are designed for production machining (automatic lathes, Swiss-type machines): their additions (sulfur, phosphorus, lead for 12L14) break up the chip, lower cutting forces and improve finish. In exchange they lose weldability and ductility.

    Comparison

    Criterion12L1412151018 (reference)
    AdditionsSulfur + leadSulfur + phosphorus (lead-free)—
    Machinability (B1112 = 100, distributor tables)~160–180%~135%~70–78%
    Chip controlExcellentVery goodLong chips
    WeldabilityAvoid (porosity, cracking, lead fumes)PoorGood
    Case hardeningPossible but less predictablePossible, limitedClassic
    UsesNon-critical screw-machine parts: fittings, pins, bushingsSame when lead is excludedGeneral and welded parts

    When to choose them

    • Large runs of turned parts where cycle time drives price.
    • Parts that aren’t welded and don’t see severe impact or fatigue.
    • 1215 when a requirement (customer, environment) excludes lead.

    When to avoid them

    • Any welded or brazed part.
    • Safety, impact or fatigue parts: prefer a heat-treated alloy steel (see 4140 vs 4340) or 1144 per the specification.
    • Food contact or potable water with lead: check applicable requirements.

    Caution

    Machining or heating 12L14 releases lead into chips and fumes: hygiene, ventilation and chip handling per health and safety rules (see Safety and CNESST).

    Machining in practice

    • Higher speeds and feeds than 1018, per tool makers’ data (see Feeds and speeds).
    • Very good surface finish in turning; ideal for Swiss-type work (see Turn-mill and Swiss-type).
    • Lead doesn’t stop tool wear: track tool life (see Tool life).

    Checklist

    • The part won’t be welded or brazed
    • No “lead-free” requirement (otherwise 1215)
    • Loading compatible (no critical impact or fatigue)
    • Material certificate if traceability is required
    • Compare published prices in the price comparison tool

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Welding a 12L14 bracketPorous, cracked weld
    Swapping 1018 for 12L14 without asking the designerReduced ductility and impact resistance
    Mixed bars in stockA 12L14 part welded by mistake; mark bar ends

    FAQ

    How do I recognize 12L14 in stock?

    Not by eye: identify bars (end colour code, tag, certificate). A test weld isn’t a method.

    Is 1215 as fast as 12L14?

    A little slower per published tables, but it avoids lead.
