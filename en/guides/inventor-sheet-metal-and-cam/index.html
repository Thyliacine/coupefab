    Autodesk Inventor: sheet metal rules, flat pattern, DXF and Inventor CAM

    Sheet metal styles and unfold rules (K-factor, bend table), creating the flat pattern, exporting the flat face to DXF with layers, and where Inventor CAM sits compared to Fusion.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page

    2. 2. Model and unfold
    3. 3. Export to DXF
    4. 4. Inventor CAM
    5. Checklist
    6. FAQ
    7. For Michaël to review

    Inventor handles sheet metal with centralized rules: once the shop’s rule is defined, every flat pattern uses it. That step decides whether bent parts come out to size.

    1. Sheet metal and unfold rules

    In Sheet Metal Defaults, you choose:

    ItemContents
    Sheet metal ruleMaterial, thickness, bend radius, reliefs
    Unfold ruleLinear K-factor, bend table or custom equation

    Shop tip

    Create one rule per material / thickness / tooling combination, with values measured by your bender. See Bend allowance and K-factor.

    2. Model and unfold

    1. Create the part from the Sheet Metal template.
    2. Native features: Face, Contour Flange, Flange, Hem, Bend.
    3. Create Flat Pattern; fix any reported errors.
    4. Check the flat pattern orientation and size.

    3. Export to DXF

    1. In the flat pattern, right-click the face (or “Flat Pattern” in the browser) → Save Copy As / Export Face As DXF.
    2. Choose the DXF version.
    3. Layer tab: turn the outer profile, inner profiles, bend up and down lines, and etching on or off, and name their layers.
    4. Turn off layers not needed for cutting (tangent lines, bend centrelines if not wanted).

    Caution

    Exporting bend lines on the same layer as the cut may get them cut. Keep them on dedicated layers or turn them off.

    4. Inventor CAM

    Inventor CAM (from Inventor HSM) builds CAM into Inventor, with logic close to Fusion: setup, 2D / 3D operations, simulation, posting with the same Autodesk post library. It is offered with the Product Design & Manufacturing Collection; check which level your subscription includes.

    Checklist

    • Shop unfold rule applied
    • Flat pattern without errors
    • DXF: cut, bend and etch layers separate
    • 1:1 scale checked in a viewer

    FAQ

    Inventor or Fusion for a small shop?

    Inventor is strong in large assemblies and production sheet metal; Fusion is more affordable and builds CAM into the same environment. The choice depends mostly on your customers and their file formats.
