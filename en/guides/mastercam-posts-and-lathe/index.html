    Mastercam: machine definition, post processor and first steps in turning

    How Mastercam links machine definition, control definition and post processor, what to ask your reseller, and the basic turning workflow: stock, chuck, roughing, finishing, grooving, threading.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Advanced DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page
    1. The posting chain
    2. What to ask your reseller
    3. Turning workflow
    4. Watch-outs on the lathe
    5. FAQ
    6. For Michaël to review
    7. Sources

    In Mastercam, G-code does not come “from the toolpath”: it goes through a chain of three elements. Understanding that chain avoids most first-program surprises.

    The posting chain

    ElementRole
    Machine definitionAxes, travel, components (turret, spindle, tailstock)
    Control definitionControl behaviour: arcs, compensation, cycles, formats
    Post processorTranslates the intermediate file (NCI) into G-code for your control

    The toolpath produces an NCI (neutral intermediate data); the post turns it into a program. Changing machines means changing the group’s machine definition.

    Caution

    Mastercam posts are usually supplied and maintained by the reseller. Ask for a post for your exact model and control (for example Haas, Fanuc 0i-TF, Okuma), and prove it on a simple first part. See Post processors.

    What to ask your reseller

    • Machine model, control and options (subprograms, canned cycles, C axis, live tooling)
    • Expected header format (program name, comments, tools)
    • Supported drilling and turning canned cycles (G71, G76…)
    • Handling of constant surface speed (G96) and spindle speed limit (G50 or G92 depending on control)
    • A test program supplied with the post

    Turning workflow

    1. Lathe machine group with the right definition.
    2. Stock properties: diameter, length, face; chuck and jaws; tailstock if needed.
    3. Geometry: half-profile in the turning plane (Z along the part, X on diameter or radius depending on setup).
    4. Operations in the usual order: Face, roughing (Rough or Dynamic Rough), Finish, Groove, Thread, Cutoff.
    5. Verify in simulation, then post.

    Watch-outs on the lathe

    PointWhy
    Diameter or radius in XA mistake doubles dimensions
    Speed limit with G96Without a limit, the spindle speeds up dangerously near centre
    Tool nose radiusCompensation (G41/G42) or compensated path, not both
    Clearance at the end of a grooveAvoid rubbing on exit

    See also CNC lathe for the basics.

    FAQ

    Can I edit a post myself?

    Technically yes (.pst files), but an uncontrolled post edit can produce a dangerous program. Keep the original, version your changes and dry-run.
