    Fundamentals · materials

    Metal grades in the shop: steel, stainless, aluminum, copper, brass, bronze

    A36, 44W, 1018, 4140, A500, 304, 316, 17-4PH, 5052, 6061, C110, C360, C932: what each grade is for, how it cuts, bends, welds and machines, linked to published prices.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    This guide follows the catalogue of a real Quebec shop (the one our price tool tracks): carbon and alloy steels, structural steels, stainless, aluminum and copper alloys. Exact mechanical properties come from the standards (ASTM, CSA, AA) and mill certs; here we talk about use and shop behaviour.

    Structural steels (shapes, plate, tube)

    GradeStandardWhere you see itIn the shop
    A36ASTM A36Plate, shapes, flat barThe reference “mild steel”: weldable, cuts well with plasma and oxy-fuel
    44W / 50WCSA G40.21Plate and shapes in CanadaThe number is the minimum yield in ksi (44 or 50); a common Canadian structural grade
    A500 gr. B / CASTM A500Structural tube (HSS), square, rectangular, roundCold-formed, welded; gr. C is stronger
    A53 / A106ASTM A53, A106PipeA106 for high-temperature service; sold as pipe, not sheet
    A513 / A519ASTM A513, A519Mechanical tube (DOM, seamless)Tighter tolerances than structural

    Carbon and alloy steels (bar, machining)

    GradeCharacterTypical use
    1018 / 1020Low carbon, machinable, weldableGeneral machined parts, fixtures; “CR” = cold rolled (better finish and tolerance), “HR” = hot rolled
    1045Medium carbonShafts, stronger parts; can be hardened
    12L14Leaded, free-machiningVery machinable, poor weldability
    4130 / 4140 / 4340Chrome-moly (plus nickel for 4340)Highly stressed parts; “HT” = pre-hardened (heat treated)
    O1Oil-hardening tool steelTooling, punches, blades

    Caution

    Welding 4140 or 1045 generally requires preheat and a suitable procedure; 12L14 welds poorly. Follow a qualified procedure (CSA W47.1 / W59 in Canada for structures).

    Stainless

    GradeFamilyIn the shop
    304 / 304LAustenitic 18-8The most common; “L” = low carbon, better after welding; work-hardens when machined
    316 / 316LAustenitic with molybdenumBetter chloride resistance (marine, food, chemical)
    303Austenitic with sulphurFree-machining 304; reduced weldability
    410 / 416MartensiticHardenable, magnetic; 416 = free-machining
    17-4PHPrecipitation hardeningHigh strength; condition (H900, H1150…) changes hardness and toughness
    Inconel 600Nickel alloyHigh temperature; costly and hard to machine

    On plasma, stainless is best cut with the gases and consumables the power source maker specifies; on air, the cut face oxidizes. Stainless cutting and welding fumes contain chromium: see Safety.

    Aluminum

    GradeCharacterTypical use
    3003Non-heat-treatable, very formableLight sheet work, covers, simple tanks
    5052 (often H32)Non-heat-treatable, good corrosion resistanceThe bent-sheet grade: bends well, welds well
    6061 (often T6)Heat-treatableMachined plate and bar, structure; bends poorly at tight radii in T6
    6063Heat-treatable, extrusionArchitectural extrusions

    Shop tip

    For a bent part, 5052-H32 is the default; for a machined part, 6061-T6. Bending 6061-T6 at a tight radius often ends with a crack on the outside of the bend.

    Copper, brass, bronze

    GradeCommon nameIn the shop
    C110ETP copperElectrical conductor (bus bar); gummy to machine; strongly reflects laser
    C360Free-cutting brassThe machinability benchmark; fittings, turned parts
    C932SAE 660 bearing bronzeBushings and bearings
    C954Aluminum bronzeHeavily loaded bushings, wear resistance

    How to choose

    1. Function: load, corrosion, temperature, conductivity.
    2. Process: bending (5052 over 6061-T6), welding (304L over 303), machining (12L14, C360).
    3. Availability and price: compare in the price tool — sheet, plate, bar and tube.
    4. Certification: ask for the mill cert if the part is structural or regulated.

    FAQ

    A36 or 44W?

    Both are common structural steels; 44W is the Canadian CSA grade, A36 the ASTM grade. Many products are dual-certified: check the cert.

    Why does stainless “stick” when machining?

    Austenitics (304, 316) work-harden: a rubbing tool hardens the surface. Enough chip, a sharp tool, no dwelling mid-cut. See Feeds and speeds.
