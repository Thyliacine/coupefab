    Troubleshooting · machining

    Machining troubleshooting: chatter, tool breakage, poor finish, drifting sizes

    Symptom → cause → fix for milling and routing: vibration, broken tools, built-up edge, poor surface finish, chip recutting and sizes that change during a run.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page

    2. Tool breakage
    3. Poor surface finish
    4. Chips and evacuation
    5. Sizes drifting during a run

    7. FAQ

    In machining, a defect rarely has a single cause. The method: start from the symptom, check causes from most to least likely, change one thing, recut.

    Chatter (vibration)

    Chatter leaves regular patterns on the surface and a characteristic high-pitched sound.

    CauseFix
    Too much tool stick-outReduce stick-out to the minimum needed
    Part poorly held, thin wallBetter clamping, supports, operation order (leave material for rigidity)
    Too much radial engagementConstant-engagement paths (Dynamic, Adaptive)
    Spindle speed in an unstable zoneShift rpm by a few percent, at constant chip load
    Worn or poorly clamped holderClean tapers and collets, replace the collet
    Low-rigidity machine (router, small mill)Less engagement rather than blindly less feed

    Tool breakage

    WhenLikely cause
    On the plungeStraight plunge with a non-centre-cutting end mill; ramp or helix needed
    Mid-passRecut chips (evacuation), overload, chatter
    In an inside cornerEngagement spikes in the corner; round the path, reduce engagement
    On first contactWrong length comp, wrong offset — see Work offsets
    Small drillNo pecking (G83), feed too high

    Poor surface finish

    • Built-up edge (material welded to the edge, especially aluminum): higher cutting speed, polished or suitable coated tool, lubrication.
    • Chip too thin (rubbing): increase chip load — see chip thinning in Feeds and speeds.
    • Worn tool: chipped or rounded edge.
    • Witness marks: continuous finishing pass, tangent arc entry.

    Chips and evacuation

    A chip left in the pocket gets recut: heat, poor finish, breakage. Air or coolant aimed into the cut, stepped evacuation, up-cut bits on routers for deep slots.

    Sizes drifting during a run

    SignCause
    Sizes change graduallyTool wear → wear offset
    Sizes change in the morning then settleMachine thermal growth → spindle warm-up
    Random sizesPart moving in the vise, chips under the part, loose stop

    Procedure

    1. Listen and look: sound, chips, edge condition.
    2. Check workholding and tool stick-out.
    3. Check parameters against the tool sheet.
    4. Change one variable, recut, note.

    FAQ

    Should I slow down when it chatters?

    Not automatically. Reducing radial engagement, shortening the tool or shifting rpm slightly often fixes it better than reducing feed, which can thin the chip and make the tool rub.
