    Plasma troubleshooting: dross, bevel, top spatter, arc loss

    Identify the dross type (low speed, high speed, top spatter), read the cut angle, correct height, speed and current in small steps, and fix arc loss and fast wear — based on Hypertherm’s guides.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page
    1. First: the four basic checks
    2. Reading dross
    3. Reading the cut angle (bevel)
    4. Arc loss and interrupted cuts
    5. Fast consumable wear
    6. Troubleshooting procedure
    7. FAQ
    8. For Michaël to review

    Most plasma cut defects come from three variables: speed, height (arc voltage) and current — plus consumable condition. Hypertherm says so clearly in its cut-quality guides. This guide follows their logic: identify the symptom, then correct one variable at a time, in small steps.

    First: the four basic checks

    1. Correct cut direction (profiles clockwise, holes counter-clockwise with a standard swirl ring) — see Lead-ins and pierce.
    2. Right process for material and thickness: exact chart row, right gases, right consumable part numbers.
    3. Consumables in good shape — see Consumables.
    4. Torch square to the plate, plate flat and well grounded.

    Reading dross

    TypeWhat it looks likeMain causeFix (Hypertherm)
    Low-speed drossThick, bubbly bead under the cut; comes off in big chunks; vertical lag linesSpeed too slow (also: current too high, torch too low)Increase speed in 5 ipm steps; raise height by 1/16 in or 5 V; lower current in 10 A steps
    High-speed drossSmall, hard, thin bead, hard to remove; “S” lag linesSpeed too fast (also: current too low, torch too high)Check nozzle wear first; lower speed in 5 ipm steps; lower height; raise current without exceeding 95% of the nozzle rating
    Top spatterFine droplets on the top edge, easy to removeWorn nozzle, speed too fast, torch too highCheck the nozzle; reduce speed and height

    Between the extremes lies a dross-free window: narrower on stainless and aluminum, wider on mild steel. A little dross in corners is normal, as speed drops through turns.

    Shop tip

    Change one variable at a time, cut a test line, note the result. Changing speed and height together teaches you nothing.

    Reading the cut angle (bevel)

    ObservationLikely causeFix
    Negative angle (part wider at top)Torch too lowRaise height (or voltage)
    Positive angle (part wider at bottom)Torch too highLower height (or voltage)
    Bevel on one side onlyTorch not square, or wrong cut directionSquare the torch; reverse direction
    Bevel growing during the jobConsumables wearing (voltage rises)Replace; adjust voltage

    Hypertherm notes that arc voltage must be readjusted as consumables wear to keep the same height.

    Arc loss and interrupted cuts

    • Speed far too fast or slow: the arc goes out (not enough metal to sustain a transferred arc).
    • Ground: loose clamp, rusty plate, slat insulated by slag.
    • Air: pressure drops during the cut, water or oil in the air.
    • THC diving or climbing: lock it in corners and holes, set the speed cutoff.
    • Pierce height or delay wrong — see Lead-ins and pierce.

    Fast consumable wear

    SymptomFrequent causes
    Electrode pits fastWet or oily air, too many starts, arc held off the plate
    Nozzle orifice ovalPiercing too low, plate contact (THC), current above rating
    Spatter stuck to the shieldPierce height too low, delay too short

    Troubleshooting procedure

    1. Do the four basic checks.
    2. Identify the dross type or angle on a straight test cut.
    3. Correct the main variable (often speed) in small steps.
    4. If no effect, correct height, then current.
    5. Still no effect: replace electrode and nozzle, check the air.
    6. Save the working combination in the CAM tool library.

    FAQ

    Why more dross near the end of the sheet?

    The plate heats up during the job; Hypertherm notes more dross may form on later cuts. Spread cuts across the sheet (Nesting).

    Does the grade matter?

    Yes: chemistry, surface condition and flatness change the dross-free window, even at the same thickness.
