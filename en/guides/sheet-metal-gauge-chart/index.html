    Sheet metal gauge chart: steel, galvanized, stainless and aluminum in inches and mm

    The same gauge isn’t the same thickness across materials: 16 ga = 0.0598 in steel, 0.0635 in galvanized, 0.0625 in stainless and 0.0508 in aluminum. Full chart, pitfalls and ordering tips.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 4 min read Level : Beginner DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    Gauge is an old convention: the bigger the number, the thinner the sheet. Most importantly, each material family has its own table. That is the number one source of ordering and quoting errors. This is the table our price comparison tool uses to convert gauges published by suppliers.

    Remember

    • Galvanized: steel thickness plus the zinc coating, hence values slightly above black steel.
    • Aluminum: Brown & Sharpe table (the same as AWG wire); many suppliers sell aluminum in decimals instead (0.040, 0.063, 0.080, 0.125 in).
    • Tolerance: actual thickness varies around nominal per the product standard (ASTM A1008/A1011 for steel, etc.); for a critical part, measure with a micrometer.
    • Beyond about 3/16 in, it’s plate and is ordered by fraction or decimal, not gauge.

    Ordering without mistakes

    • Always write gauge + decimal on the PO (“14 ga (0.0747 in) A1011”)
    • Specify material and grade (hot-rolled pickled, cold-rolled, G90 galvanized, 304 2B…)
    • Check thickness on receipt (micrometer or caliper)
    • Use the same actual thickness in CAM (K-factor, kerf) — see Bending: K-factor and deduction

    FAQ

    Why is 11 ga stainless exactly 1/8 in?

    The stainless table uses round fractions for some gauges (11 ga = 0.1250 in), while the carbon steel table historically derives from weight per square foot.

    How do I compare prices published in different gauges?

    Bring everything back to price per pound or per square foot at actual thickness: that’s what the price comparison tool does.
