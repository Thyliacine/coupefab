    Torch height control (THC) troubleshooting: hunting, diving or climbing torch

    Why the THC hunts, dives in corners or over voids, or lifts the torch: arc voltage, anti-dive, activation delay, worn consumables, grounding. A step-by-step diagnostic method.

    Reviewed November 26, 2026 3 min read Level : Intermediate DraftWritten by CoupeFab from the official documents cited. Technical review by Michaël Brassard pending.

    On this page
    1. Symptom → cause → fix
    2. Diagnostic method
    3. Checklist
    4. Common mistakes
    5. FAQ
    6. For Michaël to review
    7. Sources

    Torch height control (THC) holds a constant arc voltage; since voltage rises with arc length, it moves the torch up or down to keep the setpoint. But it’s an indirect height measurement: anything that changes voltage without changing height fools the THC. Avid CNC sums up the typical cases well: lead-ins/outs, tight corners, small holes, and moving over voids.

    Symptom → cause → fix

    SymptomLikely causeFix
    Torch dives in cornersMachine slows, voltage rises, THC goes downVelocity-based anti-dive (corner hold); threshold near cut speed depending on the control
    Dives over an already-cut part (void)No metal under the torch, voltage climbsVoltage-change anti-dive (void sensing), cut order, avoid crossing voids
    Constant oscillation (hunting)THC gain/speed too high, electrical noise, unstable setpointReduce sensitivity, check ground and wiring, filtering per the manufacturer
    Torch slowly climbs during the cutWorn consumables (voltage rises with wear), setpoint too highReplace electrode/nozzle; back to the chart
    Torch drags on the plateSetpoint too low, warped sheet, THC too slowBack to chart voltage, more responsive material profile
    Dive at the lead-inTHC activated too early, while speed buildsActivation delay after pierce (THC delay)
    Erratic behaviour everywherePoor grounding, damaged ground cable, HF interferenceDirect ground on table and plate, separated cables, manufacturer’s procedure

    Diagnostic method

    1. Back to basics: new consumables, plasma source chart (current, height, voltage, speed); see Reading cut charts.
    2. Check IHS (initial height sensing): pierce height depends on good sensing (ohmic or float).
    3. Cut a straight line on a flat sheet with THC on: if it already oscillates, it’s tuning or electrical.
    4. Add corners and small holes: if it only dives there, it’s anti-dive.
    5. Cut across an already-cut slot: tests void detection.
    6. Read the log if the control has one (Mach4 at Avid, QtPlasmaC) and mark problem spots.

    Shop tip

    Inhibit THC on small holes and small details: speed is never stable there. SheetCam can do it with path rules (see Torchmate and Avid CNC), QtPlasmaC with hole settings (see LinuxCNC / QtPlasmaC).

    Checklist

    • New consumables matching the chart
    • Target voltage = chart (adjusted only after testing)
    • THC activation delay after pierce set
    • Anti-dive enabled (velocity and/or void)
    • THC inhibited on small holes
    • Ground checked (table, plate, power source)

    Common mistakes

    MistakeConsequence
    Raising sensitivity to “follow” the sheetOscillation
    Same voltage from new to worn electrodeHeight drifts
    THC active for the whole programDives at holes and corners
    Voltage divider misconfiguredWrong reading, inconsistent THC

    FAQ

    Should THC always be used?

    Not on very small parts or very thin, flat sheets, where fixed height after sensing can be enough. On large warped sheets, yes.

    My displayed voltage isn’t the chart’s: is that wrong?

    Not necessarily: divider, wiring and wear change the reading. What matters is cut quality and actual height (check with a gauge).
